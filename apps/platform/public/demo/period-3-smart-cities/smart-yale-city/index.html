About Smart Yale City

An imaginary city designed by students; not connected to any real university.

City at a glance

Where: flat, near the coast · about 31.5 inches of rain and snow a year

Population: 15,564 to 15,657 · Motto: “Light and truth”

Big goal: the slide was not filled in yet (see the questions below).

Problems the designers want to solve

  1. Not having school: kids and teens and adults not getting educated.
  2. Tsunamis and hurricanes: not being able to protect civilians.
  3. Not having many places that people can work at: people not being able to pay the bills!

What the designers planned

  • Smart solar panels that turn to face the sun (and catch light reflected from the Moon)
  • Self-driving motorcycles and cars that drive to your phone’s location; motorcycles deliver food
  • “Vantage Point” scouting: a drone that checks the streets, and an app that detects emergencies so emergency services can get there fast
  • Smart sewers that sense the amount of waste and control the output; waste goes to the farms as fertilizer
  • Shelters, schools, workplaces, homes and emergency power storage
  • Connected story: “When the smart solar panels get energy, then it powers the motorcycles.”
  • Map key: energy zone, transportation, safety services, water and parks, homes and schools

Challenges they thought about

  • Cost: have a big agency to support the city, and slightly higher prices.
  • Privacy: for privacy systems only, the password will be long and finger ID needed.
  • If it breaks: have emergency power in storage until we get primary systems back online.

Why live in SYC?

  1. SYC has good education.
  2. We have advanced stuff.
  3. We have a lot of safety (like shelters and stuff).

Questions for the designer

  • Your Big Goal slide is still blank. In one or two sentences, what makes SYC smart, and why would people want to live there?
  • SYC has 15,564 to 15,657 people. How did you pick those numbers, and why is it a range instead of one number?
  • Your solar panels also catch light reflected from the Moon. Moonlight is sunlight bouncing off the Moon, and it is very weak. Where does SYC get most of its power at night?
  • The self-driving cars and motorcycles drive to your phone. What happens for someone who doesn't have a phone, or whose phone battery is dead?
  • When a hurricane or tsunami is coming, how does everyone find out, and how many people can fit in the shelters?
  • What new jobs will SYC create so more people have places to work and can pay the bills?
