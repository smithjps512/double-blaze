Game Studio

Six weeks, thirty classes, for sixth grade period 3. The class voted for this unit 15 to 3. In their words: "I like coding games", "a fun playable game made by me", "fun with friends". This unit takes that seriously and gives them the part of making games that decides whether the game is any good: thinking it through first.

By the end, every studio has:

  • a design doc in Game Studio: a pitch, players, a core loop, a world, mechanics, and levels written as player stories with checks and scenarios
  • a v1 game built by Double Blaze from that doc, playtested by the class
  • one mechanic of their own, built by hand in MakeCode Arcade
  • a v2 game built from their own change requests, verified against their own checks, and launched on /play

Everything they already know comes along: purpose is impact (a game's purpose is the feeling it gives the player), there is always more than one user, user stories, checks, Given/When/Then scenarios with a happy path and a bad day, the Definition of Done, and QA reports.

Studios and roles

Teams of 3 or 4 are called studios, formed on day 5 around pitches. Studio names only, never a student's name, the same rule as Launch Pad.

Every studio has four roles. Roles rotate every Monday (days 6, 11, 16, 21 and 26), so everybody does every job at least once. In a studio of 3, the Producer is also QA Lead.

RoleOwnsAsks
ProducerThe design doc and the plan. Types in /studio. Says what is done."Is it done, by our Definition of Done?"
Game DesignerMechanics, levels, balance."Is it fun? What does the player feel?"
DeveloperChecks and scenarios, with an eye on what can be built. MakeCode in week 4."Could somebody build this from what we wrote?"
QA LeadPlaytests, bad days, QA reports."What happens if the player does something weird?"

Print the role cards from day 1 and keep them on the desks.

What the teacher needs before day 1

  • Chromebooks, one per student, plus at least one per studio that can stay signed in to /studio.
  • Game Studio open on the projector with a demo studio signed in (period 3, team name Demo Studio). Nothing you do to it touches a real studio.
  • /play checked: it is where v1 and v2 appear in weeks 5 and 6.
  • Three games the class can play in the first two days. Any free browser game the school allows is fine. The class's own W.C.O 27 and Survival on /play work well: they were built from 6th grade writing.
  • Sticky notes, index cards and markers for the paper prototype on day 10.
  • The day before week 4: open arcade.makecode.com on a student Chromebook, start a new project, and press the play button on the simulator. If the school filter blocks it, ask for it to be unblocked that day, not on day 16.

One rule for the six weeks

If it is not in the design doc, it is not in the game. Double Blaze builds from what the studio wrote and nothing else, the same rule as the prototype generator. A studio that wants a better game has one lever: write a better doc. When a playtest finds something missing, the fix is a story, a check or a scenario, never "can you just add".
