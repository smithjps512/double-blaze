# Day 3: Player types, and the feeling as purpose

No em dashes anywhere in this document or any copy it generates.

At the end of today, every student can name at least two kinds of players for one game idea and say the feeling the game is for.

## Again (5 minutes)

1. Draw a core loop for any game, from memory, in under a minute.
2. Circle the core verb.

## Watch (5 to 10 minutes)

The teacher does this on the projector, start to finish. Nobody types until it
is done.

1. **Remind** them: on Launch Pad, the purpose was the impact, not what the
   app does. Say: for a game, the purpose is **the feeling it gives the
   player**. Not "it has dragons". "You feel brave."
2. **Write** four player types on the board: **Explorers** (want to find every
   secret), **Competitors** (want to win and beat a score), **Collectors**
   (want to get them all), **Builders** (want to make their own thing).
3. **Ask** for hands: who is mostly an explorer? A competitor? Show that the
   room is mixed. Say: there is always more than one kind of player, the same
   way an app always had more than one user.
4. **Add** a fifth on the side: **whoever says yes**. A parent who decides if
   the game is allowed, a teacher. Ask: what does a parent want from a game?
   (Safe, not forever, no spending money.)
5. **Take** yesterday's runner loop. Write one sentence of purpose for it: "You
   feel fast and lucky." Show how each player type gets something different
   from the same loop.

Say out loud: a game's purpose is the feeling. Every kind of player should be able to find that feeling somewhere in the game.

## Do (25 to 30 minutes)

Same pairs.

1. *(again)* Take your made up game loop from yesterday. Tidy it to 3 or 4 verbs
   and circle the core verb.
2. **(today)** Write its purpose as a feeling: "You feel ___ because ___."
3. **(today)** Pick at least two player types it is for. For each, one sentence:
   what they want from it.
4. **(today)** Add the person who says yes. One sentence: what would make them
   say yes?
5. **(today)** Read your purpose sentence to another pair. They tell you the
   feeling back in their own words. If they say a different feeling, fix the
   sentence.

Stuck? Finish the sentence "When I win this game, I feel ___." That word is usually the purpose.

## Done when

Every pair has a game idea with a feeling sentence and at least two player types, each with what they want.
