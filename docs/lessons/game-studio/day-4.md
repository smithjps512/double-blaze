# Day 4: Writing a pitch

No em dashes anywhere in this document or any copy it generates.

At the end of today, every student has written a pitch for one game on one index card, ready to say out loud in 30 seconds.

## Again (5 minutes)

1. Say your game idea's feeling sentence out loud to your pair, from memory.
2. Name two player types it is for.

## Watch (5 to 10 minutes)

The teacher does this on the projector, start to finish. Nobody types until it
is done.

1. **Write** the pitch frame on the board:
   **It's a ___ game where you ___.** Then: **You'll feel ___.** Then:
   **It's for ___ and ___.**
2. **Fill it in** for W.C.O 27, out loud: "It's a soccer obby game where you
   jump through an obstacle course to win player cards for your dream team.
   You'll feel like a pro. It's for collectors and competitors."
3. **Show** a bad one: "It's a really cool game with lots of stuff." Ask what
   is wrong. (No verbs, no feeling, no player.) Say: a pitch with no verbs is
   not a game yet.
4. **Show** the back of the card: draw one picture of the most exciting
   moment in the game. Say: one picture, the moment you want everyone to
   remember.

Say out loud: a pitch is a promise. If you cannot say it in 30 seconds, you do not know the game yet.

## Do (25 to 30 minutes)

On your own today. Everybody writes a pitch, even if you plan to join somebody
else's studio.

1. *(again)* Look at your pair's notes from days 2 and 3. Pick the idea you
   like best, or start a new one.
2. **(today)** Front of the index card: the three sentences from the board.
   Use real verbs from your loop.
3. **(today)** Back of the card: one drawing of the best moment.
4. **(today)** Say your pitch to two different people. Time it. Under 30
   seconds.
5. **(today)** After each try, ask your listener: "What will I be doing in this
   game?" If they cannot answer with a verb, change the card.

Stuck? Start with the core verb. "It's a ___ game where you [core verb] ___."

## Done when

Every student has an index card pitch they can say in under 30 seconds, and a listener can name the verb.

Pitch day. The best pitches become studios.
