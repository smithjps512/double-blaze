# Day 25: Balance as numbers

No em dashes anywhere in this document or any copy it generates.

At the end of today, every studio has written its game's key numbers and a difficulty curve across its levels.

## Again (5 minutes)

1. Name one must you sent for v2, and the check that will prove it is fixed.

## Watch (5 to 10 minutes)

The teacher does this on the projector, start to finish. Nobody types until it
is done.

1. **Write** on the board the numbers in Demo Studio's game: hero speed, jump
   height, number of hearts, enemies per level, time limit.
2. **Draw** a difficulty curve: level 1 to level 3 on the bottom, how hard on
   the side. Show a good one (rising, with a small dip before the hardest
   part) and a bad one (flat, or a cliff on level 1).
3. **Ask**: from yesterday's playtests, where did players quit? Mark it on the
   curve. Say: that is where the numbers are wrong.
4. **Change** one number in a check on the projector: "4 enemies" becomes "3
   enemies" in level 2. Say: balance is changing numbers on purpose and
   checking what happens.

Say out loud: balance is a promise to the player: hard enough to feel proud, never so hard they quit.

## Do (25 to 30 minutes)

1. *(again)* Open your studio. Are all levels still green after yesterday?
2. **(today)** List every number in your game: speed, jump, hearts, enemies,
   timers, points.
3. **(today)** Draw your difficulty curve on paper, level by level.
4. **(today)** Mark where playtesters got stuck or quit. Decide: too hard, too
   easy, or just right?
5. **(today)** Write the numbers you would change in the notes for v3. Do not
   change v2's doc; it is already being built.

Stuck? Look at the playtest notes. Wherever a player said "this is impossible" or "this is boring", there is a number to change.

## Done when

Every studio has a list of its numbers, a drawn difficulty curve, and the changes it would make next written down.
