# Day 8: World and story

No em dashes anywhere in this document or any copy it generates.

At the end of today, every studio's World tab has a hero, a goal, an obstacle, a setting and a story intro of 2 or 3 sentences.

## Again (5 minutes)

1. Say your core loop as verbs, out loud, as a studio, in order.
2. Say how you win and how you lose.

## Watch (5 to 10 minutes)

The teacher does this on the projector, start to finish. Nobody types until it
is done.

1. **Open** Demo Studio's **World** tab. Fill in the hero ("a small robot"),
   the goal ("get home before the battery dies"), the obstacle ("a junkyard
   full of magnets"), and the setting ("a city dump at night").
2. **Write** a story intro of 2 sentences: "Bolt fell off the truck on the way
   to the recycling plant. Now Bolt has to cross the junkyard before the
   battery runs out."
3. **Point** at how the story explains the loop: the battery is why the player
   collects, the magnets are why they dodge. Say: a good story gives the verbs
   a reason.
4. **Show** a story that fights the loop: a peaceful garden story for a
   dodging game. Ask what feels wrong.

Say out loud: the story is the reason for the verbs. If the story and the loop disagree, the player feels it.

## Do (25 to 30 minutes)

1. *(again)* Open your studio. Read the Core loop out loud. Is the core verb
   marked?
2. **(today)** World tab: hero, goal, obstacle, setting.
3. **(today)** The story intro: 2 or 3 sentences. Somebody who is not the
   Producer writes the first draft out loud while the Producer types.
4. **(today)** Check each loop verb against the story: why does the hero do
   this verb? If there is no reason, change the story or the verb.
5. **(today)** The Developer asks: can every part of the world be drawn on one
   screen? If not, which part comes first?

Stuck? Start the intro with "One day, ___" and end it with "Now ___ has to ___."

## Done when

Every studio's World tab is full, and the story gives at least two of the loop's verbs a reason.
