# Day 10: A paper prototype, played by another studio

No em dashes anywhere in this document or any copy it generates.

At the end of today, every studio has watched another studio play its game on paper and written down three things it learned.

## Again (5 minutes)

1. Read your mechanics out loud as a list: name and rule.

## Watch (5 to 10 minutes)

The teacher does this on the projector, start to finish. Nobody types until it
is done.

1. **Show** a paper prototype of Demo Studio's game: a level drawn on paper,
   the hero as a sticky note, enemies as index cards, a coin as a marker dot.
   Say: no screens today. Paper is how studios test ideas before they spend
   time building.
2. **Play** it with a volunteer. The volunteer moves the hero. You are the
   computer: you read the rules from the Mechanics tab and move the enemies.
   Say: the computer only does what the rules say.
3. **Stop** when a rule does not cover what happened ("what if I jump on the
   magnet?"). Write it on the board. Say: that question is a gift. It is
   something the design doc forgot.
4. **Show** the three questions for the studio to write down after: What was
   fun? What was confusing? What did our rules forget?

Say out loud: the computer only does what the rules say. Every "what if" you could not answer is a gap in your doc.

## Do (25 to 30 minutes)

1. *(again)* Open your studio. The Producer has the Mechanics tab on screen to
   read from.
2. **(today)** Build your paper prototype: one level on paper, the hero, the
   obstacles. 10 minutes.
3. **(today)** Swap with the studio next to you. One of their players plays
   your game. Your QA Lead reads your rules; your Developer moves the world.
   Nobody explains anything that is not written down.
4. **(today)** Write every "what if" on a sticky note.
5. **(today)** Swap back. As a studio, answer the three questions: fun,
   confusing, forgotten. Fix one mechanic in Game Studio.

Stuck? If you are not sure what the computer would do, the answer is: nothing. Write that down as a gap.

## Done when

Every studio has three answers written down, and at least one mechanic changed in Game Studio because of the playtest.
