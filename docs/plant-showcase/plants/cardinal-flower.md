## About

Cardinal flower is a native perennial that sends up tall spikes of the most
intense red of any wildflower in North America. It grows wild along streams,
springs, swamps and low wet woods across much of the continent.

### Fun facts

- It is named after cardinals. Not the birds, the Roman Catholic cardinals, whose
  robes are the same bright red.
- Hummingbirds do almost all the work. The flowers are long red tubes, and most
  insects cannot get down them. Ruby-throated hummingbirds can, so the plant
  depends on them to move its pollen.
- It blooms late, in the back half of summer, which makes it an important meal
  when most other native flowers have already finished.
- It likes wet feet. Most garden flowers hate soggy soil, and this one seeks it
  out, which is why it turns up at the edges of ponds and rain gardens.
- It grows 2 to 3 feet tall, and sometimes reaches 4.

## Care

1. Light. Full sun to partial shade. Some afternoon shade helps in hot weather.
2. Soil. Rich in organic matter and consistently moist. This is one of the few
   plants that is happy in a spot that stays damp.
3. Water. Do not let it dry out. In hot, dry stretches it needs watering, and a
   layer of mulch helps hold the moisture in.
4. Where to put it. Pond edges, rain gardens, low damp corners, and anywhere the
   soil stays wetter than the rest of the yard.
5. Lifespan. Each plant is fairly short-lived, but it reseeds itself, so a patch
   keeps going even as individual plants come and go.
