## About

The common blue violet is a small native perennial that grows in woods, thickets
and along streambanks through eastern and central North America. It is the plant
most people mean when they find violets in a lawn.

### Fun facts

- The flowers and young leaves are edible. People put the flowers on salads and
  desserts, and cook the young leaves like greens.
- It is a nursery for butterflies. Fritillary butterflies lay their eggs on
  violets almost exclusively, and the caterpillars of the great spangled
  fritillary, the meadow fritillary and the variegated fritillary all eat the
  leaves.
- It has secret flowers. Besides the purple ones you can see, violets make
  closed flowers near the ground that never open and pollinate themselves. That
  is how a patch spreads even in a bad year.
- It can grow where almost nothing else will, including wet soil, heavy clay,
  and near black walnut trees, which poison most other plants.
- It spreads so well that some people call it a weed. It is a native wildflower
  that happens to be very good at its job.

## Care

1. Light. It likes dappled shade best but will grow in almost any light.
2. Soil. Moist and rich is ideal, though it tolerates clay and wet ground that
   would kill most plants.
3. Water. Keep it from drying out. The leaves tend to look rough by late summer
   in the heat, which is normal.
4. Spreading. It self-seeds freely and spreads by underground stems. Pull the
   extras if you want it to stay in one place.
5. Problems. Slugs, snails and aphids sometimes chew on it, and powdery mildew
   can show up. None of it usually kills the plant.
