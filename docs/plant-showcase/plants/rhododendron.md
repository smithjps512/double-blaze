## About

Rhododendrons are popular, colorful spring flowers. They are famous for their
beautiful flowers, and they are low maintenance.

- Rhododendrons are evergreen or deciduous shrubs.
- They get big. They can range up to 20 feet tall and 15 feet wide.
- They are toxic to humans, pets and livestock.

### History

Rhododendrons date back over 55 million years, and their recorded human history
spans ancient honey mishaps and 19th century plant hunting.

## Care

1. Light. Give them partial sun, ideally morning sun with shade in the
   afternoon. Hot afternoon sun scorches the leaves.
2. Soil. They want slightly acidic soil, around pH 4.5 to 6.0, that drains well
   and is rich in organic matter. They do not tolerate heavy clay, so clay soil
   needs compost or bark worked into it first.
3. Roots. Their roots are shallow and fibrous, which means they sit near the
   surface and dry out faster than most shrubs. Plant them shallow rather than
   deep.
4. Water. Keep the soil consistently moist, especially through summer. Water
   deeply and less often rather than a little every day, and let the top inch of
   soil dry slightly in between.
5. Mulch. Put down 2 to 3 inches of bark, straw or other organic mulch to hold
   moisture and keep the roots cool. Keep the mulch a few inches back from the
   trunk so the stem does not rot.
6. Pruning. They need very little. Take out dead, damaged or diseased branches,
   and do any pruning right after the plant finishes flowering.
