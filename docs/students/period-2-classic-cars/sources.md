# Where this team's documents came from

**These documents are different from every other team's, and the team should
know that.**

Every other team in the class wrote their own plan and stories, and those were
transcribed here word for word. This team handed in one user story and no plan,
so this plan and three of these four stories are a **first draft written with
their teacher**, from what the team said they liked about cars and the two
websites they picked out:

- `howacarworks.com`
- A "how cars work, beginners guide to basic car mechanics" article on
  `fleetrabbit.com`

Both are sites that teach how a car works rather than sell cars or play games,
and that is what shaped this plan. The team's own words about what they like
("the very cool looks and engine, the mechanics, sounds, power, speed, and what
you can do to give the car more horsepower") are what each feature is built
around.

**The quiz story is theirs.** It is transcribed from
`User Story Examplequizdamien.pdf`, the one document they handed in, with the
author's own name removed from the user type and the role generalised to "a
user".

## What the team should do first

Change something. This is a draft, not a verdict. Argue with it. The parts of it
that are wrong are the most useful parts, because spotting them is the work.

Three things were deliberately left for them:

1. **The app has no name.** The prototype is titled after the team, and the
   coach notes say so. Naming it is a good first job and it is theirs.
2. **There is no sign in and no accounts**, on purpose. Nothing in this app needs
   to know who you are, and leaving it out is one less thing to build. Deciding
   *not* to build something is a real product decision.
3. **The car list and the parts list are not filled in.** Which cars, which
   parts, and what each page says is theirs to write, and it is the part they
   will enjoy.

## What changed after this was written

This team has three people and none of them is the developer, so the app was
built for them as a web site rather than by them in Anvil, and they were given
the admin console behind it instead. Running a real site is the half of the web
nobody shows a thirteen year old, and it is a better use of three people than
watching one of them type Python.

The cars, the parts and the quiz questions seeded into it are placeholders. The
parts and the questions are facts and were written in full; the cars are the
part this team is supposed to choose, so all four are flagged in the console as
theirs to replace. The horsepower figures are the manufacturers' original
factory ratings and the top speeds are approximate.

Their build cards and architecture are left in place. They are the record of
what was asked for, and every page on the site came from one of them.
