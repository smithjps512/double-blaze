# Where this team's documents came from

Transcribed from the plan the team handed in, as typed. Spelling and punctuation
corrected, and the seven features pulled out of the single paragraph they were
written in. Nothing was added and nothing was cut.

**There are no user stories yet.** That is the next assignment, not an
oversight, and the prototype says so: it has no screens to open because a plan
without stories has nothing to build from.

## What the team should read first

Their plan has more genuinely good ideas in it than the length suggests. Three
worth naming to them, because a team that knows what it did well repeats it:

1. **Real prices for real things.** One dollar for water, two for a can, three
   for a fountain drink. That is the whole purpose of the game working as a
   game mechanic: a player finds out what helping actually costs by spending it.
   Nobody else in the class connected their economy to the real world like that.
2. **"School appropriate weapons."** They set that limit themselves, before
   anybody asked. That is a real product decision about audience.
3. **A real place.** Downtown Blacksburg grounds it in somewhere they and their
   players actually know.

## The question the team has to answer

**Who are the bad guys?**

The plan says players "fight bad guys" and also "help people in need", and it
never says who the enemies are. In a game about homelessness that is not a small
gap. If a player could read the enemies as homeless people, or as a threat that
homeless people represent, the game says the opposite of what this team wants it
to say. Nobody intends that, and it is exactly the kind of thing that happens
when the enemy is left undefined.

This is the most valuable design conversation in the class, and it is theirs to
have. Whatever they decide, it belongs in the plan in one sentence.

## Two smaller things

- **"Homeless Helper God"** as the top rank. Real volunteering apps do use
  levels and badges, so the idea is sound, but it is worth them asking out loud
  whether helping people should end in becoming a god at it, and deciding on
  purpose either way.
- **Real people in a real town.** If the game shows Blacksburg, the people in it
  should be characters rather than anyone recognisable.
