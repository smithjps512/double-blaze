## Plant page
Sunchokes, also called Jerusalem artichokes, are resilient, high yielding
perennial sunflowers native to North America.
- **Tall enough to be a windbreak.** The above ground plant looks like a wild
  sunflower and easily grows 6 to 14 feet tall. It casts big shadows and can
  accidentally block the light from nearby crops.
- **Aggressive spreader.** Sunchokes spread fast through underground rhizomes.
  They are nearly impossible to get rid of once planted in the ground. Any tiny
  piece of tuber left in the soil grows into a new plant next spring.
- **The harvest is underground.** The edible parts are the knobby tubers, which
  look like ginger root. One plant can yield over a gallon of tubers.
- **Timeline.** It needs a long season, 120 to 150 days, to mature its tubers.
  It blooms bright yellow late in the season, usually August or September,
  before dying back.
### Things worth knowing
- Sunchokes store their energy as inulin rather than starch the way potatoes do.
  Inulin is a prebiotic fiber that people cannot digest but gut bacteria love,
  so eating a lot of them can cause gas and bloating.
- Freshly dug sunchokes taste better after a couple of hard frosts. Frost breaks
  the inulin down into fructose and makes the tubers sweeter and nuttier.
- The tubers have thin skins and dry out, wrinkle or rot quickly in open air.
  They keep best left in damp soil until the moment you want to eat them.
- Raw, they are crisp and sweet like water chestnuts. Boiled, roasted or baked,
  they turn creamy like a potato.
## Care page
Planted in late August in Virginia (USDA zones 6 to 7), the tubers spend fall
and winter developing roots underground rather than putting up top growth.
### Planting
1. **Use heavy containers.** A 5 to 15 gallon container or heavy grow bag at
   minimum. Never plant them straight into a greenhouse ground bed or they take
   the space over permanently.
2. **Soil mix.** Loose, well draining potting soil with compost and some sand or
   perlite.
3. **Depth and spacing.** Plant tubers 3 to 5 inches deep with the eyes facing
   up, about 12 inches apart.
4. **Water and mulch.** Water thoroughly after planting, then add a 3 inch layer
   of straw or leaf mulch.
### Through the year in the greenhouse
- **Late August to October, establish roots.** Keep the greenhouse ventilated so
  it does not overheat. Sunchokes like 65F to 90F. Water when the top 2 inches
  of soil are dry and never leave the soil waterlogged, or the tubers rot.
- **November to January, simulate winter.** The tubers need a chill cycle to
  break dormancy and sweeten, so do not heat the greenhouse. Water the bare
  minimum, just enough that the soil is not bone dry.
- **February to March, early sprouting.** As daylight increases the tubers wake
  up and push green shoots through the soil. Increase watering once shoots
  appear.
- **April onward, manage the height.** Because they shoot up past 10 feet they
  will hit the greenhouse ceiling and block light from other crops. Once the
  spring frost risk passes, usually April in Virginia, move the containers
  outside into full sun.
