## The product plan is now a draft written with your teacher

**Read this bit.** Your plan is marked `Draft: true`, which means the prototype
built from it says so on its own front page, and the reason is that this version
is not all your writing.

What you handed in was a purpose, one kind of user, and two features. There was
no product description at all. That is not enough for anybody, including you, to
build from, so your teacher wrote a fuller plan **around what you already
said**, rather than replacing it.

**Everything the plan is built on came from you:**

- "kids like me have fun while gaming and can make a cool PC that they like, for
  cheap money" is your purpose, and it is a good one. The new version says the
  same thing at more length.
- "Building your own PC on our app, choosing the design and how it looks" became
  the first two features.
- "Or you could come to our store and build it there" is still in there, as
  *Find our store*. Nobody deleted your shop.
- The engraving, "a PC that is exactly like the one that they ordered", and
  "it is all hand made with great attention to detail" came out of your user
  story. Those three are the reason somebody would buy from you instead of a
  big website, and the description is written around them.

**What was added, that you should argue with:**

- **Two more kinds of user.** Whoever actually pays, and the workshop, meaning
  you. Both are real and neither was in your plan.
- **A product description**, because the heading was empty.
- **Two features you did not list**, *Track your order* and *Find our store*.
  Both already existed in your build architecture, which had run ahead of your
  plan.

**A draft is a starting point and this one is yours to change.** Cross things
out. If you think the parent user is nonsense, say so and take it out, and say
why. That argument is worth more than the paragraph is.

## Two things for the team

1. **The product name.** Your plan said "The dream team" in one place and
   "PC BROS" in the feature column. Your teacher has been calling the project
   PC Bros, so PC BROS is the product name here and The Dream Team is the team
   name. **Confirm that is the way round you want it**, because it is on every
   page you have.

2. **Your one user story is written from the company's point of view**, not a
   customer's: "As a PC company I want to make money so that I can be rich."
   The acceptance criteria underneath are all things the company does. A story
   describes what a *user* wants. Card 1 rewrites it, and notice that the app
   you would build for the customer is a completely different app from the one
   you would build for the company.
