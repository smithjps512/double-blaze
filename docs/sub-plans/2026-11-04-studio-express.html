What is happening on their screens

What students are doing

Each student works on their own. They design a video game using what they have learned this semester (purpose, users, user stories, checks, and scenarios), then watch it get built and play it. The site asks them, step by step:

  1. Who they are: period, initials, and a studio name.
  2. Their game: a name, a genre (Platformer, Top-down quest, or Sky dodge), and the feeling it should give a player.
  3. Their hero and world: who you play and where it happens.
  4. What the hero collects, and what is in the way.
  5. A core loop: 3 verbs the player does over and over (like run, jump, collect).
  6. Mechanics: a power-up, plus numbers for speed, jump and lives.
  7. A player story: "As a player, I want to ___ so I can ___", one check (how we would know it works), and a bad day (Given / When / Then).

Then they press Build. Spark, the site's AI helper, designs their game in about 10 to 20 seconds while a build screen keeps them busy. Then they play it.

If a student asks "what do I do?" Say: "Follow the steps on the screen. The orange box tells you what is missing."

Classroom management

  • Chromebooks stay on this site. Other tabs and games are a behavior reminder.
  • Sound stays off. The games are silent unless a student turns sound on. Ask them to keep it off.
  • Initials only, no real names, and school-appropriate names. The site blocks bad words, but please redirect anyone trying.
  • Help each other, but do not type on someone else's Chromebook. Everyone builds their own game.

If something goes wrong

The site will not load, or the wifi is downHand out the paper design worksheet (last page, copy as needed). A complete worksheet counts for completion. Collect them and leave them for me.
A build takes over a minuteIt falls back to a simpler builder on its own. Ask them to wait, or press Build again.
A student finishes in 10 minutesPress Remix to improve their game (up to 3 builds), then play and review classmates' games on the finished-games page.
A student is stuck on a boxThe orange box on screen says exactly what is missing. Point them to it.
A student says their game is brokenThat is great feedback. Have them write down what they did and what happened, and leave it for me.
